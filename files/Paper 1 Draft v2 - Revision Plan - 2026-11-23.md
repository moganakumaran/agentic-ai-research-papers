## 1. The one decision still Mohan's: M4 title scope

Assistant's recommendation in chat (2026-11-23 08:28 PT, source:253) was **narrow the title**, e.g. *"Failure Memory Does Not Help Text-to-SQL Agents: A Compute-Fair, Corrected-Label Evaluation"* — because a frontier run fixes only one of four scope axes (model size; still one benchmark, one task family, specific memory designs), a 1-run frontier contrast (~$12) breaks the paper's own 4-run/pass^k standard, and the honest 4-run version (~$45+) exceeds the $24 ledger cap ($17.84 spent).

**New evidence since that recommendation, verified today (see §3):** the morning briefing's two papers jointly predict a *capability × staleness interaction* — Jayaram et al. show memory benefit is model-dependent (DeepSeek-V3.2 +9.5 pts with full guidelines; gpt-oss-120b better with a ~29-guideline subset than the full set), and Xu et al. show experienced agents fail where novices succeed after a silent tool change, with harm growing with experience. If a frontier A1 run gained while mini/Qwen null, the claim becomes "memory benefit is capability-gated" — stronger than a flat null. If it also nulls, the null generalises across capability. This is the strongest argument *for* the frontier run, and it was not in the 08:28 reasoning.

**Plan position:** Draft v2 can start under the narrowed title either way — the narrowed title is correct for the evidence in hand, and the frontier question goes in Limitations as explicit future work. If Mohan later authorises the frontier run, it becomes an amendment + a results section, not a rewrite. Do not let the title decision block the writing fixes in §2.

## 3. New citations verified today (2026-11-23, arXiv primary pages) — ready for Draft v2 §2/§6

All ten papers from the 08:32 PT morning briefing were checked against their arXiv abstracts; every number quoted in the briefing matches. The ones that matter for Draft v2:

- **StaleBench — Murshed, arXiv:2611.19752** (submitted 15 Nov 2026; accepted to Findings of EACL 2027). 892 real updating facts. Central result, verified verbatim: a retention policy and its retain-everything control return the *identical* statement at rank 1 for every test query, yet differ by **29.5 and 65.6 points** in reader accuracy on two readers when no date is shown. Contamination@5 ranks them correctly where current@1/@5 do not. **Draft v2 use:** independent precedent for the central claim — retrieval metrics can rank memory policies backwards, same phenomenon as the A3 original-label win (+9.8) vanishing on corrected labels. Cite it and report corrected labels as primary.
- **Experience Rot — Xu, Yin, Zhang, Zhao & Liu, arXiv:2611.20922** (submitted 16 Nov 2026). Abstract verified: experienced agents copy their earlier call instead of checking the current interface, even when the tool definition shows the change, then report success; failure appears in every form of memory tested and grows with experience; announcing the update barely helps. **Draft v2 use:** the silent-drift analogue for tool interfaces; supports the capability × staleness interaction framing in §6/limitations. Do *not* cite the 0.23 vs 0.63 incomplete-docs figures from secondary summaries — not verified on the primary abstract.
- **How Much Should an Agent Remember? — Jayaram et al., arXiv:2611.26361** (submitted 19 Nov 2026; IAAI 2027 oral). Verified: DeepSeek-V3.2 gains **+9.5 pts** task completion (+16.1 scenario) with its full 191-guideline set; gpt-oss-120b starts 39.9%, reaches 51.2% with all 199 guidelines but **55.4%** (mean of two runs) with a selected subset of ~29. Central finding: the right amount of memory is a property of the model, not the memory. **Draft v2 use:** the model-dependence leg of the M4 discussion.
- **WikiTrace — Lee, Choi, Baek & Hwang, arXiv:2611.14320** (submitted 10 Nov 2026). Full title: *"Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents"* — cite by that title, system name WikiTrace. Verified: names "memory-induced degradation" (agent fails tasks it would solve without memory by following a wrong lesson); evidence-linked lessons (each lesson linked to its trajectory) reduce it on five benchmarks. **Draft v2 use:** the gated/scoped-memory counterpoint — our episodic store had no evidence-linking or gating, which bounds the null and seeds Paper #2 / future work.
- **Benchmarks' Last Exam — Zhu & Cat, arXiv:2611.27622** (submitted 20 Nov 2026). Verified: 749 tasks across 8 frontier benchmarks; verified validity **92.5%** closed-form vs **29.3%** multi-step suites; **71.7%** of confirmed defects withhold credit from compliant solutions. **Draft v2 use:** one sentence in §2/§6 — benchmark validity is now being quantified, and our dual-label design is the data-agent instance of it. Note: it reviews no data-agent benchmark (gap tag).
- **RAG in a Trenchcoat — Liu et al., arXiv:2611.22179** (submitted 17 Nov 2026). Verified: minimal BRIDGE memory reaches parity within bootstrap CI with prior SOTA on LongMemEval-S and BEAM, beats best memory baseline on EverMemBench by 5.7–6.3 pts at matched reader; "what separates memory designs is not what they retrieve but what they enable an agent to do". **Draft v2 use:** supports evaluating memory by what it enables the agent to do (pass^k / task success), not retrieval metrics — our §5 framing.
- **ProbeAgent — Huang et al., arXiv:2611.26897.** Verified: 1,244 failures → 536 failure-mode descriptions across 22 configurations / 10 benchmarks; annotators accept **90.0%** of descriptions, correctly reject 83.3% of similar alternatives. **Draft v2 use (optional, §6):** failure-mode diagnosis is becoming executable, but ProbeAgent never tests whether *remembering* the modes helps — the gap our study fills. Not a core citation; keep to one sentence if used.
- Also verified, no change needed: **AskWise** (Liang et al., arXiv:2611.27474; AmbiQT recall +8.71 pts Doubao Lite / +10.00 GPT Sol — model names matter, briefing's "+8.7/+10.0" is a rounding of these); **Who Grades the Agent?** (Houlamy et al., arXiv:2611.27860; BenchJack near-perfect on 9/10 benchmarks without solving tasks; SplitBench blocked 75% of adversarial attempts); **Do Senior Agents Do Their Job?** (Xie et al., arXiv:2611.27139; planted errors adopted 2.9%, generic reviews followed in up to 71% of decisions). These three are background for the briefing, not Draft v2 citations.
